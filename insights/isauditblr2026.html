What Is an IS Audit and Who Needs One?

An Information Systems (IS) Audit evaluates the controls, security, and reliability of an organisation's IT systems and processes. In India, IS Audit is relevant for: RBI-regulated banks and NBFCs (mandatory IS audit per RBI circular); SEBI-regulated stock brokers and depositories; companies storing sensitive personal data (DPDPA compliance); IT companies bidding for government contracts; and MNCs whose group auditors require an IT General Controls (ITGC) assessment as part of the SOX or group internal audit program.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is IS audit mandatory for technology companies in Bangalore?

A: IS Audit is mandatory for RBI-regulated entities (banks, NBFCs, payment aggregators) and SEBI-regulated market intermediaries. For unlisted technology companies, IS Audit is not legally mandatory but is often required by: (a) group auditors as part of SOX ITGC testing for a foreign parent company's internal control assessment; (b) enterprise clients as a vendor security requirement; (c) DPDPA compliance programs reviewing data processing controls; or (d) insurance providers for cyber insurance policy issuance.

Q: What is the difference between an IS audit and a cybersecurity audit?

A: An IS Audit evaluates the governance, controls, and reliability of all information systems and processes — including IT General Controls, application controls, change management, and data integrity. A cybersecurity audit focuses specifically on security threats, vulnerabilities, and protective controls — penetration testing, firewall configuration, access privilege review. IS Audit is broader and governance-focused; cybersecurity audit is narrower and threat-focused. PDKA conducts both under its CISA-certified practice.
