Types of Business Entities for Bangalore Businesses

  • Private Limited Company: Most common structure for startups and foreign subsidiaries — limited liability, separate legal entity, can raise equity, MCA regulated. Minimum 2 directors, 2 shareholders.
  • Limited Liability Partnership (LLP): Preferred for professional services — flexible profit-sharing, no mandatory audit below Rs 40 lakh turnover, lower compliance cost than company.
  • One Person Company (OPC): Single founder company with limited liability. Cannot have foreign shareholders. Cannot raise VC/equity funding. Suitable for solo founders in early stage.
  • Wholly Owned Subsidiary (WOS): A Private Limited Company with 100% foreign shareholding. Standard vehicle for foreign companies entering India. Requires RBI FEMA compliance for share issuance.
  • Branch Office / Liaison Office: Not separate entities — extensions of the foreign company. Restricted activities. Approved by RBI. Not suitable for revenue-generating operations.

Bangalore Company Registration: Time and Cost

  • Private Limited Company: 7–15 working days (MCA21 portal); Rs 8,000–Rs 20,000 professional fees; government fees vary by authorised capital
  • LLP: 5–10 working days; Rs 5,000–Rs 15,000
  • Foreign subsidiary (WOS): 15–25 working days including DIN for foreign directors; additional RBI filings post-incorporation

Post-Registration Checklist for Bangalore Companies

  • PAN and TAN from Income Tax department
  • GST registration (if turnover exceeds Rs 40 lakh or making interstate supplies)
  • Professional Tax registration (Karnataka PT Act)
  • Shops and Establishments Act registration (if employing staff at a Bangalore office)
  • ESI and PF registration (if 10+ employees for ESI; 20+ for PF)
  • Import Export Code (IEC) if importing/exporting
  • FEMA filings (FC-GPR within 30 days of share allotment for foreign-owned company)

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How long does it take to register a company in Bangalore?

A: A private limited company in Bangalore can typically be registered in 7–15 working days via the MCA21 portal. The process involves: obtaining DSC (Digital Signature Certificate) for proposed directors, applying for DIN, filing SPICe+ form for name reservation and incorporation, and obtaining Certificate of Incorporation (CIN). PDKA handles the complete registration process including name search, documentation, and all MCA filings.

Q: Can a foreign national be a director of an Indian company?

A: Yes. Foreign nationals can be directors of Indian companies. They need a Director Identification Number (DIN) from MCA, which requires submission of identity and address proof apostilled by the relevant country authority. At least one director must be an Indian resident (ordinarily resident in India for at least 182 days in the previous calendar year). PDKA assists foreign directors with DIN applications and apostille requirements.

Q: What is the difference between a Branch Office and a Subsidiary in India?

A: A Branch Office is an extension of the foreign parent company — not a separate legal entity. It can conduct specified activities (manufacturing, R&D, professional/technical services) but liability flows back to the parent. A Subsidiary (typically a WOS Private Limited Company) is a separate Indian legal entity — the foreign parent's liability is limited to its shareholding. Subsidiaries have full operational flexibility and can raise debt/equity from Indian sources. Most foreign companies operating in India for revenue prefer a subsidiary over a branch.
