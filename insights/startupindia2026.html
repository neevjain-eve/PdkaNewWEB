DPIIT Recognition: Who Qualifies

  • Incorporated in India as Private Limited Company, LLP, or Registered Partnership Firm
  • Up to 10 years old from incorporation date
  • Annual turnover not exceeding Rs 100 crore in any financial year
  • Working towards innovation, development, or commercialisation of new products/services driven by technology or IP
  • Not formed by splitting or reconstructing an existing business
  • Foreign ownership percentage does not affect eligibility — 100% foreign-owned startups qualify

The Angel Tax Exemption: Why Foreign-Funded Startups Need DPIIT Recognition

Section 56(2)(viib) — the "angel tax" provision — taxes the excess of share issue price over Fair Market Value as income. Venture capital and angel investment rounds almost always value companies above book value. Without DPIIT recognition, an Indian startup raising USD 2 million at a Rs 50 crore valuation (vs. Rs 5 crore book value) faces tax on Rs 45 crore of "excess consideration." With DPIIT recognition, this tax does not apply.

3-Year Income Tax Holiday Under 80-IAC

DPIIT-recognised startups can apply separately to the Inter-Ministerial Board (IMB) for 100% income tax exemption for any 3 consecutive years within the first 10 years. IMB approval requires demonstrating genuine innovation. Not all DPIIT-recognised startups automatically get this — a separate IMB application is needed.

Other Benefits: Patents, Trademarks, Government Procurement

  • 80% rebate on patent filing fees; expedited examination (90 days vs. standard 5-7 years)
  • 50% rebate on trademark registration fee
  • Exempt from prior experience/turnover requirements in government tenders up to Rs 25 crore
  • Self-certification for 9 labour laws and 3 environmental laws

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can a foreign-owned startup in India get DPIIT recognition?

A: Yes. DPIIT recognition is available to any Indian-incorporated entity (Private Limited Company, LLP, or Partnership Firm) that meets the eligibility criteria — regardless of the nationality or percentage of its shareholders. A 100% US or Singapore-owned Indian startup can apply for and receive DPIIT recognition and all associated benefits including the angel tax exemption.

Q: What is the angel tax and how does DPIIT recognition help avoid it?

A: Angel tax (Section 56(2)(viib) of the Income Tax Act) taxes the difference between the price at which a private company issues shares and the Fair Market Value of those shares as income in the hands of the company. Since most startup investment rounds are at a significant premium to book value, this creates a substantial tax liability. DPIIT-recognised startups are exempt from this provision under a specific CBDT notification, making DPIIT recognition essential before any funding round.

Q: How do foreign investors repatriate money from an Indian startup?

A: Foreign investors can repatriate from an Indian startup through: (1) Dividends — subject to 20% withholding tax (or lower DTAA rate); (2) Share buyback — 20% buyback tax payable by the company; (3) Secondary sale to another investor — capital gains taxed in India, DTAA may provide relief; (4) Liquidation on wind-up — capital gains treatment. All repatriation events require FEMA compliance including valuation certificate and FC-TRS filing.
