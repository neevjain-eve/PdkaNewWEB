What changed

The 57th GST Council met on 8 October 2026. The Ministry of Finance press release on its recommendations covers process reforms (registration, returns, refunds and disputes), changes to input tax credit (ITC), measures on arrest, prosecution and goods in transit, and a set of rate and classification decisions. The release states that these are recommendations: they take legal effect only through amendments to the law and through notifications and circulars, which had not been located on the official sites at the time of writing.

Who is affected

Registered taxpayers, applicants for registration, exporters, businesses in inverted duty structures, e-commerce sellers and operators, and businesses that move goods with e-way bills.

Main recommendations

  • Registration: automatic amendment of registration particulars (except the principal place of business, which is also automatic for rule 14A registrants); phased automatic acceptance of cancellation applications; a new rule 14B letting small sellers without a physical presence in a State register using an e-commerce operator's warehouse as their place of business, where ITC passed on is up to ₹2.5 lakh a month.
  • Returns: changes to GSTR-1/1A/IFF and GSTR-3B to improve reconciliation, new rules 86C and 86D, and a rule 60(6A) on actions in the Invoice Management System. These are to apply from the April 2027 return, after public consultation.
  • Refunds: automatic sanction of excess electronic cash ledger balances; the acknowledgement period shortened from 15 to 10 days, with deemed acknowledgement; provisional sanction of 90% of zero-rated and inverted duty claims based on system risk evaluation; removal of the cap of 1.5 times the value of like goods in rule 89(4)(C).
  • Accumulated ITC refunds: input services in inverted duty cases for ITC availed on or after 1 November 2026; capital goods (zero-rated and inverted duty) refunded over 60 months for ITC availed on or after 1 April 2027.
  • Section 17(5): removal of the blocks on outdoor catering, health and life insurance, telecom towers, pipelines outside factory premises, free samples, and goods written off on expiry of a legally required shelf life.
  • Disputes and penalties: no show cause notice under sections 73, 74 and 74A where the tax involved is below ₹10,000; a 5% penalty in non-fraud cases if tax and interest are paid within 30 days (section 73) or 60 days (section 74A) of the order; the general penalty under section 125 reduced from ₹25,000 to ₹10,000; pre-deposit for penalty-only appeals capped at ₹40 crore.
  • Arrest and prosecution: omission of section 69 of the CGST Act (arrest) and an increase in the section 132 prosecution threshold from ₹1 crore to ₹5 crore.
  • E-way bills: interception only on specific intelligence authorised by an officer not below Joint Commissioner rank, and no interception in transit States.
  • Other items: a waiver of the late filing charge under section 39(1) for taxpayers with prior-year turnover up to ₹5 crore who file by the end of the month in which the return was due; an in-principle concept note for an optional annual return with quarterly payment for taxpayers up to ₹5 crore who make only B2C supplies; e-invoicing for certain reverse charge supplies and imports of services for taxpayers with turnover of ₹5 crore or more.
  • Rates and classification: among other items, a nil rate for psyllium seeds, a 5% option for electric vehicle passenger transport and rentals with operators, and 5% without ITC on certain e-commerce delivery services.

Key dates

  • 8 October 2026: meeting of the Council.
  • 1 November 2026: ITC availed on or after this date is relevant to the input services refund recommendation.
  • April 2027 return: proposed start for the revised returns mechanism.
  • 1 April 2027: ITC availed on or after this date is relevant to the capital goods refund recommendation.

Practical points

  • Nothing above changes a taxpayer's legal position until the amendments, notifications or circulars are issued. Their wording and effective dates will be the reference.
  • Businesses with inverted duty or export refund claims may wish to track the dates from which ITC on input services and capital goods becomes refundable.
  • The returns changes are proposed for public consultation, so details may change.
  • The full press release runs to many more items than are summarised here; the official text should be read for any specific case.
