Who Needs Transfer Pricing Documentation in Bangalore?

Any Indian company or LLP with international related-party transactions (RPTs) exceeding Rs 1 crore in aggregate in a financial year must maintain TP documentation — a Local File (Form 3CEB certified by a CA). Companies with RPTs exceeding Rs 50 crore (product transactions) or Rs 10 crore (service transactions) in a country with a CbCR treaty are additionally required to file a Master File (Form 3CEAA). Bangalore's dense GCC and MNC subsidiary population makes TP one of the highest-volume compliance areas in the city.

Most Scrutinised Transactions by CBDT's TP Division

  • Cost-plus services to foreign parent: IT captives billing at cost + 7%–15% markup — CBDT benchmarks against comparable Indian companies with higher markups
  • Royalty payments: Indian subsidiaries paying IP royalties to foreign parent — CBDT challenges the rate and the existence of unique IP
  • Management fees: Charges from parent for shared services — deductibility challenged unless specific services and benefits documented
  • Intragroup loans: Interest rate on ECB from related party — benchmarked against LIBOR/SOFR plus credit spread for comparable external debt
  • Business restructuring: Converting a full-risk entity to limited-risk entity — transfer of profit potential is taxable

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the transfer pricing markup expected for a Bangalore IT captive?

A: CBDT benchmarks typically support an arm's length markup of 12%–18% on total costs for a Bangalore IT captive providing software development services to a foreign parent. The exact range depends on the comparable set selected — Indian software companies with similar functions. Companies filing TP documentation with markups below 10% frequently attract scrutiny. PDKA's benchmarking analysis identifies and defends appropriate markup ranges.

Q: What happens if a company does not file Form 3CEB?

A: Failure to file Form 3CEB (the Transfer Pricing Accountant's Report) attracts a penalty of 2% of the transaction value under Section 271BA. More significantly, CBDT can make a TP adjustment without the benefit of the company's chosen method and benchmarking. The adjustment and associated interest can far exceed the penalty — making TP documentation a high-priority compliance area.
