When Is Statutory Audit Mandatory?

  • All companies registered under the Companies Act 2013, regardless of turnover (including Private Limited and OPC)
  • LLPs with turnover exceeding Rs 40 lakh or capital contribution exceeding Rs 25 lakh
  • Individuals/firms with turnover exceeding Rs 1 crore (Section 44AB Tax Audit)
  • Professionals with gross receipts exceeding Rs 50 lakh
  • Banks, NBFCs, and entities subject to RBI oversight have additional audit requirements

Key Statutory Audit Milestones for Companies in Bangalore

  • Financial Year End: March 31 (for April–March year companies)
  • AGM: Must be held by September 30
  • Audit Completion: Signed financial statements required before AGM — typically by August
  • AOC-4 (Financial Statements filing): Within 30 days of AGM — by October 30
  • MGT-7A (Annual Return): Within 60 days of AGM — by November 30

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is statutory audit compulsory for all private limited companies in Bangalore?

A: Yes. Every company incorporated under the Companies Act 2013 — including private limited companies — must have its accounts audited by a Chartered Accountant every financial year, regardless of turnover or profit. The auditor is appointed at the first AGM and must be ratified at each subsequent AGM. The signed audit report must accompany the financial statements filed with MCA.

Q: How long does a statutory audit take for a Bangalore startup?

A: For a startup with turnover under Rs 10 crore and a clean books position, a statutory audit typically takes 3–6 weeks from financial year end, assuming complete records are available. Delays occur due to: incomplete reconciliations, unreconciled TDS credits, missing vendor invoices, or GST mismatches. PDKA conducts a pre-audit readiness review to identify and resolve these issues before fieldwork begins, compressing the timeline.

Q: What is the difference between a statutory audit and a tax audit?

A: A statutory audit (under the Companies Act) verifies that financial statements give a true and fair view and are prepared per Ind AS/GAAP — the auditor expresses an opinion on the financials. A tax audit (Section 44AB of the Income Tax Act) verifies compliance with tax-specific requirements and reports in Form 3CA/3CD — focused on tax-relevant disclosures. Companies often require both: the statutory audit for MCA filing and the tax audit for ITR filing.
