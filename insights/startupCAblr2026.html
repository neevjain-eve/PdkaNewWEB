ESOP Taxation: What Every Bangalore Startup Founder Must Know

  • At Exercise: Difference between Fair Market Value (FMV) at exercise and exercise price = perquisite income — taxed as salary at employee's slab rate (up to 30% + surcharge)
  • At Sale: Difference between sale price and FMV at exercise = capital gain — Short-term if held less than 24 months (taxed at slab), Long-term if held 24+ months (10% for unlisted, 12.5% for listed)
  • For qualifying DPIIT startups: Tax deferral — ESOP perquisite tax is deferred to the earliest of: 5 years, sale of shares, or cessation of employment
  • TDS obligation: The company must deduct TDS on the perquisite value at exercise — even though no cash changes hands. The company pays TDS from its own funds and recovers from employee's next salary or via advance from employee

Convertible Note Compliance for Indian Startups

SEBI permits Indian startups to issue convertible notes (CNs) to investors — a hybrid instrument that converts to equity at the next financing round. CNs must be issued for a minimum of Rs 25 lakh from a single investor, must be FEMA-compliant if issued to foreign investors, and are exempt from the angel tax provisions if issued to certain investor categories. The terms (conversion discount, valuation cap, interest rate) must be documented in the CN agreement and reported to RBI within 30 days of receipt.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does DPIIT recognition help with ESOP tax for startup employees?

A: Yes. For DPIIT-recognised startups, the Income Tax Act provides a deferral of the perquisite tax on ESOP exercise. Instead of paying tax at the time of exercise, the employee pays tax at the earliest of: 48 months from the end of the assessment year in which shares are allotted, the date of sale, or the date of cessation of employment. This deferral helps employees avoid paying large tax bills when exercising options without cash liquidity.

Q: What is the angel tax and can DPIIT recognition help avoid it for investments from foreign investors?

A: The angel tax (Section 56(2)(viib)) previously applied to premium on share issuance from all investors including foreign investors — creating a major obstacle to FDI in Indian startups. Finance Act 2024 extended angel tax to foreign investment. However, CBDT subsequently issued an exemption notification covering investments from specific foreign jurisdictions and SEBI-registered funds, effectively exempting most foreign VC investments. DPIIT recognition provides a separate exemption. PDKA advises on the applicable exemption for each specific investment.
