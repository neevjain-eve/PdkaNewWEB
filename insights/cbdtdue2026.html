What changed

On 28 September 2026, the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) announced, through a press release, an extension of two due dates for Assessment Year (AY) 2026-27, which corresponds to Financial Year 2025-26. Both extensions relate to taxpayers who are subject to audit under the Income-tax Act, 1961.

  • The "specified date" for furnishing the tax audit report has been moved from 30 September 2026 to 21 October 2026.
  • The due date for furnishing the return of income has been moved from 31 October 2026 to 21 November 2026.

The press release states that a formal order or notification will be issued separately. Readers should refer to that instrument once it is available, as it will set out the legal terms of the extension.

Who is affected

The press release applies the extended return date to persons listed at serial number 2 in the table below Explanation 2 to section 139(1) of the Act. In general terms, this is the category of taxpayers whose accounts are required to be audited under the Act. The press release does not reproduce the table, so the exact categories should be confirmed from the section and the formal order.

The press release does not mention transfer pricing reports or any other filing category. Taxpayers in other categories, including those who are not required to have their accounts audited, should not assume that these extended dates apply to them, and should check their own statutory due date.

Key dates

  • Announcement: 28 September 2026.
  • Tax audit report (AY 2026-27): extended from 30 September 2026 to 21 October 2026.
  • Return of income for the audit category: extended from 31 October 2026 to 21 November 2026.

Practical points

  • Check the category first. The extension is tied to a particular class of taxpayers under section 139(1). Whether a person falls within it depends on the facts of that person's case and the wording of the formal order.
  • Audit report comes first. The tax audit report is generally required to be furnished before the return is filed. The shorter gap between 21 October and 21 November may affect how filing work is planned.
  • Look at the formal order. The press release says a separate order or notification will follow. Any condition or detail in that document would take precedence over a summary such as this one.
  • Other dates are not covered by the press release. It does not address advance tax instalments, interest for late payment of tax, or the due dates of taxpayers in other categories. These should be checked separately against the Act and official notifications.
  • Keep the records ready. Books of account, reconciliations and supporting schedules needed for the audit report and the return are usually prepared in advance, whatever the final due date turns out to be.
