What Is the DPDPA?

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA) is India's first comprehensive data protection law. Full enforcement begins May 13, 2027, with penalties of up to ₹250 crore per breach. Every organisation that processes "digital personal data" of Indian residents — whether in India or abroad — must comply.

Step 1: Data Mapping & Inventory

The foundation of DPDPA compliance is understanding what personal data you hold, where it lives, and how it flows. Your data map should capture:

  • Categories of personal data collected (name, contact, financial, health, biometric)
  • Purpose of processing for each data category
  • Data flows — internal teams, third-party processors, cross-border transfers
  • Retention periods and disposal mechanisms

Step 2: Consent Management

DPDPA mandates free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous consent before processing personal data. Key implementation requirements:

  • Consent notice must be in clear, plain language — not buried in terms and conditions
  • Consent must be as easy to withdraw as it is to give
  • A "Consent Manager" (registered with the Data Protection Board) may be used for managing consent at scale
  • Children's data (under 18) requires verifiable parental consent

Step 3: Data Principal Rights

Every individual whose data you process has rights under the DPDPA that you must operationalise:

  • Right to access — summary of personal data and processing details on request
  • Right to correction and erasure
  • Right to grievance redressal — you must appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) or grievance officer
  • Right to nominate — individuals can nominate another person to exercise rights in the event of death or incapacity

Step 4: Breach Notification

In the event of a personal data breach, the Data Fiduciary (your organisation) must notify:

  • The Data Protection Board of India — within 72 hours of becoming aware
  • Affected Data Principals — in prescribed format, without undue delay

Step 5: Vendor & Cross-Border Compliance

Data processors (vendors handling data on your behalf) must be contractually bound to DPDPA obligations. Cross-border data transfers are permitted only to countries notified by the Central Government — watch for the whitelist notification expected in 2026.

Penalties at a Glance

  • Failure to implement adequate security safeguards: up to ₹250 crore
  • Failure to notify breach: up to ₹200 crore
  • Non-fulfilment of Data Principal rights: up to ₹50 crore
  • Violation of children's data provisions: up to ₹200 crore

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When did the Digital Personal Data Protection Act come into force in India?

A: The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDPA) received Presidential assent on August 11, 2023. However, the operative provisions are being brought into force in a phased manner — the government notifies the date of commencement for each section. Key sections dealing with consent, data principal rights, and obligations of data fiduciaries were expected to be notified in 2024-25. The Data Protection Board of India (DPBI) is yet to be fully constituted and operationalised as of mid-2026.

Q: Does DPDPA apply to B2B data processing or only consumer data?

A: DPDPA applies to "personal data" — data about an identifiable individual. This includes: consumer data (names, emails, purchase history), employee data (payroll, HR records, performance data), and any other individually identifiable data processed in a B2B context. B2B company data (company names, GST numbers, corporate emails) that is not linked to an individual may fall outside DPDPA scope. However, contact persons' data within B2B relationships is personal data and is covered.
