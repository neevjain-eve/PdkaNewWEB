Why Transfer Pricing Is the #1 Tax Risk for MNC Subsidiaries in India

CBDT raised Rs 14,000+ crore in transfer pricing adjustments in FY 2024-25. Every Indian subsidiary that transacts with its parent, sister company, or any group entity is exposed. Inadequate documentation attracts penalties of 2% of transaction value — before any tax adjustment. For a subsidiary with Rs 50 crore in intercompany transactions, that is Rs 1 crore in penalty on the documentation alone.

Which Transactions Require TP Documentation?

  • Software/IT development services rendered to parent (most common for Bangalore GCCs)
  • Management fees, shared services charges, and IT infrastructure costs allocated by parent
  • Royalties and licence fees for use of parent's IP, brand, or technology
  • Interest on intercompany loans and guarantee commissions
  • Import/export of goods between group entities
  • Cost contribution arrangements (R&D pooling)

Three-Tier Documentation Framework

Local File (Form 3CEB): Required for international transactions exceeding Rs 1 crore. Functional analysis, comparables, method, arm's length price. Filed by September 30 each year.

Master File (Form 3CEAA): Required if group consolidated revenue exceeds Rs 500 crore. Covers group value chain, IP policy, financing. Filed by September 30.

CbCR (Form 3CEAD): For Indian parent entities of MNE groups with consolidated revenue above Rs 5,500 crore. Foreign MNCs with Indian subsidiaries generally file CbCR in their home country.

CBDT's Highest-Scrutiny Areas in 2025-26

  • IT captive centres with TNMM margins below 15% — CBDT using internal comparables to reject low-margin benchmarks
  • Management fees without specific service evidence — any charge above 5% of cost base being questioned
  • ESOP cost add-back — CBDT inflates the cost base by adding back stock compensation, raising the margin denominator
  • Marketing intangibles — Indian distributors building local brand are allegedly under-compensated

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the penalty for not maintaining transfer pricing documentation in India?

A: 2% of the value of each international transaction for failure to maintain documentation, and a separate 2% for failure to report. If CBDT makes a TP adjustment exceeding Rs 1 crore, an additional penalty of 100%–300% of the tax on the adjusted amount applies. Interest under 234B and 234C also runs on tax shortfalls from TP adjustments.

Q: What is the most common transfer pricing method used for IT services companies in India?

A: The Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM) using Operating Profit / Total Cost (OP/TC) as the profit level indicator is the most commonly used method for IT captive service providers and GCCs in Bangalore. CBDT expects OP/TC margins of 15%–25% for routine IT development services. Companies benchmarked below this range face TP additions.
