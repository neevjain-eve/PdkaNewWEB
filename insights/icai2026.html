Why the 2026 Ethics Update Matters

The Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) revised its Code of Ethics in 2026 to align with the International Ethics Standards Board for Accountants (IESBA) 2024 handbook. These are not cosmetic changes — they affect how CA firms in Bangalore and across India can advertise, accept clients, charge fees, and handle independence situations for audit engagements.

Key Changes: Advertising and Publicity

  • Website and social media: CA firms may now maintain professional websites and LinkedIn profiles listing services, team credentials, and client sectors — but no testimonials, rankings, or comparative claims against other firms.
  • Prohibited: Fee comparisons, "Best CA firm" claims in paid advertising, cold-calling corporates, and paying referral fees to non-CAs.
  • Permitted: Publishing articles, whitepapers, and educational content (like this one) is explicitly encouraged as professional development communication.

Independence for Audit Engagements

  • Long association threat: A partner who has been the engagement partner for the same audit client for 7+ consecutive years must rotate. Firms must maintain rotation logs.
  • Non-audit services: Providing bookkeeping, payroll, or tax filing for an audit client creates a self-review threat. The 2026 update tightens the safeguard requirements — a documented "cooling off" review by an independent partner is now required.
  • Fee dependency: If fees from one audit client exceed 15% of total firm revenue for two consecutive years, the firm must disclose this to the client's Those Charged with Governance (TCWG) and apply enhanced safeguards.

Networking and Referral Arrangements

CA firms may now enter into formal referral arrangements with other professional firms (lawyers, company secretaries, cost accountants) provided: (a) no financial consideration passes for referrals, (b) the client is informed of any referral relationship, and (c) the arrangement does not impair independence for audit clients.
