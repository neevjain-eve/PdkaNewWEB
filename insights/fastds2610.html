What changed

The Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers Disclosure Scheme, 2026 (FAST-DS) is a one-time, voluntary disclosure scheme under Chapter IV (sections 130 to 144) of the Finance Act, 2026, read with the FAST-DS Rules, 2026. According to the Income Tax Department's FAQ document, it commenced on 16 August 2026 and declarations can be filed online until 31 December 2026. The valuation date for assets is 31 March 2026.

Eligible persons can declare certain undisclosed foreign assets and foreign income on payment of a specified amount. The FAQ states that this gives immunity from further tax, penalty and prosecution under the Black Money Act, 2015 for the items declared.

Who is affected

The scheme is open to persons resident in India under section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 in the relevant previous year. A non-resident or a resident but not ordinarily resident person can also declare if they were resident in the year to which the undisclosed income relates, or in the year the asset was acquired. Two categories are set out in section 133:

  • Category A: undisclosed foreign assets or income not offered to tax. The combined value of the asset (as on 31 March 2026) and the income must not exceed ₹1 crore. The amount payable is tax at 30% plus an additional amount equal to that tax, effectively 60%.
  • Category B: foreign assets whose income was already offered to tax, or which were acquired while the person was non-resident, but which were not shown in the return's foreign asset schedule. Aggregate value must not exceed ₹5 crore. The amount payable is a flat ₹1 lakh.

The FAQ's illustration for Category A: a ₹60 lakh foreign bank account plus ₹20 lakh of undisclosed foreign income gives ₹80 lakh, and the amount payable at 60% is ₹48 lakh.

Key dates and process

  • 16 August 2026: scheme commenced.
  • 31 March 2026: valuation date.
  • 31 December 2026: last date to file a declaration.

The process is online. The declaration is made in Form 1 with supporting documents and valuation reports. Per the FAQ, a payment order (Form 2) is issued within one month from the end of the month of declaration, and payment is due within two months from the end of the month in which that order is received. A further period of up to two months is available with simple interest at 1% per month or part of a month, subject to an outer limit of four months. Payment is intimated in Form 3 and certified in Form 4.

Exclusions

The scheme does not cover income or assets representing proceeds of crime where proceedings under the Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002 have been initiated or are pending, or an assessment year for which assessment under the Black Money Act, 2015 is already complete. Assets other than bank accounts may be valued under Rule 3, with a variance of up to 20% from the declared value permitted under Rule 5(2).

Practical points

  • Check residential status for the relevant years against section 6.
  • Identify the category for each asset, based on whether its income was offered to tax and on the value thresholds.
  • Allow for the payment window. It runs from the Form 2 order, so a declaration close to 31 December leaves less time to arrange payment.
  • Assemble valuation records, as these are uploaded with Form 1.
