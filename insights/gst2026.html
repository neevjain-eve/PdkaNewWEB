Overview

GSTR-9 (Annual Return) and GSTR-9C (Reconciliation Statement) for FY 2025-26 are due by December 31, 2026. With GST scrutiny intensifying, errors in the annual return have become the primary trigger for departmental notices. This guide walks you through the filing process and highlights the most frequent problem areas.

Key Changes for FY 2025-26

  • Table 8A auto-population: GSTR-9 now auto-populates ITC eligible as per GSTR-2B; manual overrides are flagged for scrutiny
  • ITC reversal disclosures: Enhanced disclosures required for Rule 42/43 reversals (proportionate ITC on exempt supplies)
  • HSN-wise outward supply: Mandatory for taxpayers with annual turnover above ₹5 crore (6-digit HSN)
  • GSTR-9C self-certification: Taxpayers above ₹5 Cr turnover must file a self-certified reconciliation — CA certification no longer mandatory but recommended

Most Common Reconciliation Errors

Recurring issues commonly seen in GSTR-9 filings:

  • GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B mismatch: Amendments filed in later months create differences between declared and paid tax; all amendments must be captured in Table 9/10
  • ITC in books vs GSTR-2B: ITC claimed in books but not appearing in GSTR-2B due to supplier non-filing leads to reversals and interest liability
  • RCM non-disclosure: Reverse charge mechanism transactions on import of services are frequently omitted from Table 6B
  • Advance receipts: GST on advances received but invoice not issued in the same year creates a reconciling item that must be separately disclosed

Pre-Filing Checklist

  • ☑ Reconcile turnover as per books with GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B
  • ☑ Verify ITC availed in GSTR-3B against GSTR-2B for all 12 months
  • ☑ Identify and reverse ineligible ITC (Section 17(5) blocked credits)
  • ☑ Confirm HSN summary is accurate for all outward supplies
  • ☑ Reconcile e-way bills with invoices for high-value outward supplies
  • ☑ Review any pending refund claims and ensure they are reflected correctly

Penalties for Non-Filing

Late filing of GSTR-9 attracts a fee of ₹200 per day (₹100 CGST + ₹100 SGST), capped at 0.25% of turnover in the state. Additionally, discrepancies between GSTR-9 and GSTR-3B exceeding thresholds can trigger scrutiny notices under Section 61.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the penalty for late GST return filing?

A: Late fee for GSTR-3B is Rs 50 per day (Rs 20 per day for nil returns), subject to a maximum of Rs 10,000 per return. Interest at 18% per annum applies on any net tax liability for the delayed period. For GSTR-1, the late fee is Rs 50 per day (nil: Rs 20), max Rs 10,000. Repeated late filing also affects the ITC available to your customers — damaging business relationships.
