Purpose of this note

Companies in India that have foreign shareholders or overseas investments deal with several regulators, each with its own portal, forms and dates. This note summarises three recurring dates that are stated on official government or Reserve Bank of India (RBI) pages. It is not a complete calendar, and dates set by notification can change, so each item should be checked against the current official source.

Advance tax

The Income Tax Department's FAQs on interest under sections 234A to 234D set out the advance tax schedule. Payments are due on 15 June (at least 15% of the tax), 15 September (at least 45%), 15 December (at least 75%) and 15 March (100%). The same page states that any tax paid on or before 31 March is also treated as advance tax paid during the financial year. The page lists the interest provisions that apply to shortfalls and delays, which are in sections 234B and 234C.

The Income-tax Act, 2025 now appears on the Department's website alongside the 1961 Act. Readers should confirm which Act and which section applies to the year they are dealing with.

RBI Foreign Liabilities and Assets (FLA) return

According to the RBI's FAQ, the FLA return is to be filed by Indian-resident entities that have outstanding foreign direct investment or overseas direct investment at the end of March of the previous or current year. The FAQ lists companies, LLPs, SEBI-registered AIFs, partnership and proprietary firms among the entities covered. Entities with no such investment at either date, or that have only received share application money, are not required to file.

  • Due date: 15 July of the reporting year.
  • Provisional figures: the FAQ states entities may file on provisional or unaudited figures, and must request RBI permission to file a revised return once audited figures are ready.
  • How to file: through the RBI's FLAIR portal after registering as a new entity user.
  • Late filing: the FAQ states that filing after the due date is treated as a FEMA violation, and that penalty provisions may apply, with a late submission fee payable through the Foreign Exchange Department of the entity's regional office.

GST annual return (GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C)

The GST portal's FAQs state that registered normal taxpayers file GSTR-9, with composition taxpayers filing GSTR-9A instead and certain categories, such as casual and non-resident taxable persons, not filing. GSTR-9C applies to registered persons whose aggregate turnover exceeds the threshold notified by the government from time to time; it involves audited annual accounts and a reconciliation statement. The due date is 31 December following the financial year, or a later date if extended by notification. A late fee applies to GSTR-9 filed after the due date.

Related income tax dates for audit cases

For Assessment Year 2026-27, a CBDT press release of 28 September 2026 extended the tax audit report date to 21 October 2026 and the return of income date to 21 November 2026 for persons in the audit category listed at serial number 2 under Explanation 2 to section 139(1). Other categories should check their own due dates.

Practical points

  • Confirm each date on the current official page before relying on it, since extensions are made by notification or circular.
  • Maintain one list of due dates per regulator (Income Tax Department, RBI, GST, Ministry of Corporate Affairs) so that each filing is tracked against its own source.
  • Keep records of investment positions as on 31 March, as the FLA return refers to that date.
