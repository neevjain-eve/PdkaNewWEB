Why IT Companies Face Unique GST Challenges

IT and software companies operate at the intersection of domestic B2B, domestic B2C, and export transactions — each governed by different GST rules. Mis-classification of the place of supply or failure to claim export benefits can result in significant tax leakage or blocked ITC.

Place of Supply: Getting It Right

For IT services, the place of supply determines whether GST is charged at all and which state's SGST applies. Key rules:

  • B2B (GSTIN customers): Place of supply is the recipient's registered location. IGST applies for inter-state; CGST+SGST for intra-state
  • B2C (unregistered customers): Place of supply is the location of the service provider — important for SaaS platforms serving individual consumers
  • Export of services: Place of supply is outside India; transaction is zero-rated. Ensure the recipient is outside India AND payment is received in foreign exchange (except Nepal/Bhutan)

Maximising Export Benefits

IT companies exporting services have two options for handling GST on their costs:

  • LUT (Letter of Undertaking): Export without paying GST and claim full ITC refund on inputs. This is generally the preferred route — no cash flow blockage
  • Export with IGST payment: Pay IGST on exports and claim a refund. Useful only if the company has insufficient ITC and prefers a clean cash refund

ITC Optimisation for IT Companies

Common areas of ITC leakage in IT companies:

  • Cloud infrastructure (AWS, Azure, GCP): IGST paid on these invoices is fully creditable — ensure vendor invoices carry your GSTIN
  • Software licences: SAC code 9972 — eligible for ITC if used in the course of business
  • Office rent: GST on commercial leases is creditable; residential accommodation leased for employees is blocked under Section 17(5)
  • Business travel: GST on business-class air travel is blocked under Section 17(5); economy class is creditable

Refund Claims: Common Rejection Grounds

GST refund claims for IT exporters are frequently rejected or delayed due to:

  • FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate) not matching invoice amounts due to forex fluctuation — use BRC/FIRC with bank endorsement
  • Services not qualifying as "export" due to the recipient having a PE in India
  • Mismatch between turnover declared in GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is GST applicable on IT services exported from India to foreign clients?

A: IT services exported from India to foreign clients are zero-rated supplies under the IGST Act — GST is not charged on the invoice. The Indian IT company can claim a full refund of all Input Tax Credit (ITC) paid on inputs used for those exports. To export without paying GST, the company must file a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) at the start of each financial year. Without an LUT, the company must pay IGST on the export invoice and then claim a refund — creating cash flow issues.
