What Is PE and Why Does It Cost So Much?

A Permanent Establishment (PE) in India subjects a foreign company's India-sourced profits to Indian corporate tax at 40% — instead of 0% for a non-resident with no PE. A single wrongly-structured employee in India can create PE exposure running into crores. India's tax treaties and CBDT's aggressive PE assertions make this the single biggest hidden tax risk for foreign companies operating in India.

Four Types of PE That CBDT Asserts

  • Fixed Place PE: A dedicated office, factory, server, or even a regularly used desk in a co-working space. Sharing a server room with the Indian subsidiary has been held as PE in specific cases.
  • Agency PE: An Indian person (employee or subsidiary) who habitually concludes contracts or plays the principal role in negotiating contracts on behalf of the foreign company. This is the most commonly triggered PE for foreign companies with India-facing business development staff.
  • Service PE: Under most Indian DTAAs (USA, UK, Singapore, Japan), providing services in India for more than 90 days in any 12-month period creates Service PE — even without a fixed place. Covers seconded employees, on-site consultants, and project delivery teams.
  • Construction PE: Any building site, installation, or supervisory activity lasting beyond the treaty threshold (typically 6–12 months).

High-Risk Activities — Red Flags for CBDT

  • Foreign parent executives visiting India quarterly to "review" subsidiary operations — CBDT may characterise this as management control = Agency PE
  • Business development employees based in India negotiating deals for the foreign parent
  • Indian subsidiary routinely signing contracts that are later formally executed by the parent
  • IT consultants deployed at client sites in India for more than 90 days on behalf of a foreign company
  • Foreign company maintaining a server in India to deliver digital services

PE Risk Mitigation Checklist

  • Ensure Indian subsidiary has genuine independence — own management, board resolutions, arm's length contracts
  • Track cumulative days of foreign parent employees in India per tax year — alert at 80 days
  • Review all contracts: the Indian entity must not sign on behalf of the parent
  • Maintain strong Transfer Pricing documentation characterising the Indian entity as a limited-risk service provider — contradicts Agency PE assertion
  • Use a Liaison Office only during pre-market exploration; switch to WOS before any revenue activity begins

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can a foreign company have a PE in India if it only has a subsidiary there?

A: A subsidiary is a separate legal entity and does not automatically constitute a PE of the parent. However, if the subsidiary acts as an agent of the parent — habitually concluding contracts on the parent's behalf — CBDT can assert Agency PE. The key test is whether the subsidiary has genuine independence in its operations. Proper TP documentation and operational separation reduce PE risk significantly.

Q: How many days can a foreign company's employee work in India before creating PE?

A: Under most India DTAAs (USA, UK, Singapore, Japan), a Service PE is created when the foreign company's employees provide services in India for more than 90 days in any 12-month period. Some treaties use a 183-day threshold. The 90-day count is cumulative across all employees visiting for the same project, not per individual — making it easy to breach without a tracking system.

Q: What tax rate applies to PE income in India?

A: PE income is taxed at the foreign company rate of 40% (plus surcharge and health and education cess, making the effective rate ~43%). This compares with 22% or 15% for an Indian subsidiary. PE attribution also requires a separate TP-like exercise to determine how much of the foreign company's global profit is attributable to the India PE — a complex and disputed calculation.
