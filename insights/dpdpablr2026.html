DPDPA 2023: Key Obligations for Bangalore Tech Companies

  • Notice and Consent: Collect personal data only with explicit, informed consent. Consent notice must be clear, in plain language, and in at least one of 22 scheduled languages if requested by data principal.
  • Purpose Limitation: Data collected for a specific purpose cannot be used for another purpose without fresh consent.
  • Data Minimisation: Collect only data that is necessary for the stated purpose.
  • Data Retention: Erase personal data when the purpose for which it was collected is fulfilled — cannot retain indefinitely.
  • Data Principal Rights: Right to access, correction, erasure, and grievance redressal. Must be fulfilled within specified timelines.
  • Data Fiduciary Obligations: Significant Data Fiduciaries (SDFs) — to be notified by the government — will have additional obligations: Data Protection Officer, annual audit, DPIA (Data Protection Impact Assessment).

Cross-Border Data Transfers Under DPDPA

DPDPA permits cross-border transfer of personal data to countries notified as safe by the Government of India — the whitelist approach. Until the whitelist is published (expected in 2025-26), companies should document existing data flows and build consent frameworks that are transfer-aware. Bangalore GCCs and IT companies sending data to parent companies in the US, UK, EU, and Singapore need to map these flows and assess which consent or contractual mechanism applies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does DPDPA apply to foreign companies processing Indian personal data?

A: Yes. DPDPA has extraterritorial scope — it applies to processing of personal data of Indian residents, regardless of where the processing occurs. A US company processing data of Indian users on servers in the US is subject to DPDPA. This means any company serving Indian customers or handling Indian employee data must comply, regardless of where they are incorporated or where their servers are located.

Q: What are the penalties for non-compliance with India's DPDPA?

A: DPDPA penalties are among the highest in India's regulatory landscape: up to Rs 250 crore (approx. USD 30 million) for failing to take reasonable security safeguards resulting in a data breach; up to Rs 200 crore for failure to notify the Data Protection Board in case of a breach; up to Rs 50 crore for violating children's data protection requirements. The Data Protection Board (DPB) is the enforcement authority. Penalties are per contravention, not per person affected.
