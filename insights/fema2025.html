Overseas investment by Indian entities

Overseas direct investment by Indian residents is governed by the Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Rules, 2022 (G.S.R. 646(E), 22 August 2022) and the Reserve Bank's Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Regulations, 2022 (FEMA 400/2022-RB, 22 August 2022), together with the RBI's Master Direction on Overseas Investment.

  • Financial commitment limit: an Indian entity may make financial commitments within the overall limit set in Schedule I of the Rules. A financial commitment above USD 1 billion (or equivalent) in a financial year needs prior RBI approval.
  • Annual Performance Report (APR): the RBI's instructions for the APR form say it is due by 31 December. Where the foreign entity's accounting year ends on 31 December, it is due by 31 December of the next year.
  • Step-down subsidiaries: the APR instructions require the acquisition, setting up, winding up or transfer of a step-down subsidiary, and any change in the foreign entity's shareholding during the reporting year, to be reported. Not reporting this is treated as non-submission of the APR.

Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)

According to the RBI's FAQ, resident individuals, including minors, can remit up to USD 250,000 per financial year (April to March) for permissible current or capital account transactions, or a combination of both. The limit covers foreign exchange bought or remitted from all sources in India during the year.

Tax collected at source on remittances

The Income Tax Department's TCS rates page lists tax collection at source on LRS remittances and on overseas tour packages. It states that no tax is collected on an LRS remittance of up to ₹10 lakh, and that for purposes other than education or medical treatment tax is collected at 20% of the amount above ₹10 lakh. The page does not state the rate for education or medical remittances above that amount, and rates have been the subject of recent amendments. The current rate for each purpose should therefore be checked on the Income Tax Department's website or in section 394 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 before any remittance is made.

TCS is generally adjustable against the remitter's final tax liability and is not by itself an additional final tax.

Practical points

  • Entities with overseas subsidiaries can compare their step-down holdings and shareholding changes with what the APR requires.
  • Individuals can track cumulative remittances against the annual USD 250,000 limit across all banks.
  • Rules for exports of goods and services were replaced by new RBI regulations in force from 1 October 2026, so earlier export-reporting practices should be checked against the current text.
