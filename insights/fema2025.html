Key FEMA Updates for FY 2025-26

The Reserve Bank of India made several significant amendments to FEMA rules in 2025 that affect IT exporters, foreign-funded startups, and MNC subsidiaries in Bangalore and across India. Here are the most impactful changes and what they mean for your compliance calendar.

Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) — Revised Framework

  • Automatic route limit raised: Indian companies can now invest up to 400% of their net worth overseas under the automatic route (up from 300%) — beneficial for IT companies setting up sales offices in the US, UK, or Singapore.
  • Step-down subsidiary reporting: All indirect overseas subsidiaries (subsidiaries of subsidiaries) must now be reported in the Annual Performance Report (APR) filed by July 31 each year.
  • Startup exception: DPIIT-recognised startups can invest overseas up to USD 1.5 million per year under the automatic route without the net worth constraint — enabling faster global expansion.

LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme) Updates

  • TCS at 20% now applies on LRS remittances exceeding Rs 7 lakh per year for most purposes (travel, investment, gifts). IT companies must track employee LRS usage to avoid TCS on business travel reimbursements being misclassified as personal LRS.
  • LRS remittances for education and medical treatment continue at 5% TCS above Rs 7 lakh.
  • Overseas credit card spends are now included in the LRS limit — companies must brief employees who hold company credit cards used abroad.

SOFTEX Filing: Still the Most Missed Obligation for IT Companies

Every Bangalore IT company that exports software services must file SOFTEX forms — either through STPI (for STPI-registered units) or directly through the AD bank. The filing must be done within 30 days of raising the export invoice. Common gaps: companies that deregistered from STPI but continue exporting; companies that merged or restructured without updating SOFTEX registration; and IT companies billing through overseas subsidiaries (SOFTEX still required for the Indian entity's portion).

15CA/15CB: Mandatory for All Foreign Remittances Above Threshold

Form 15CA (online declaration) and Form 15CB (CA certificate) are required for most outward foreign remittances from India. The CA must certify: the nature of payment, applicable DTAA rate, TDS deducted, and that FEMA conditions are met. Errors in 15CB certification expose both the CA and the remitter to penalty.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is SOFTEX and which IT companies in India must file it?

A: SOFTEX (Software Export Declaration) is an RBI-mandated form that IT and software companies must file for every export invoice for software services rendered to foreign clients. All Indian IT companies exporting software — whether STPI-registered or not — must file SOFTEX within 30 days of raising the export invoice. Failure attracts FEMA penalties and compounding by RBI.

Q: Is Form 15CB required for all foreign payments from India?

A: Form 15CB is required for most remittances to non-residents where the payment is chargeable to tax in India. Exempt remittances include: salary of non-resident individuals where TDS has been deducted, payments covered by specific RBI exemptions, and payments below Rs 5 lakh if taxable but the remitter furnishes Form 15CA Part A. A practising CA must sign Form 15CB after reviewing the transaction.

Q: What is the penalty for not filing FEMA returns on time?

A: FEMA violations attract compounding by the RBI Compounding Authority. The compounding amount is typically 0.5%–1% of the contravention amount, subject to a minimum of Rs 5,000 and maximum of Rs 2 lakh per violation. Additionally, CBDT may disallow foreign remittances in the income tax assessment if FEMA filings are deficient.
