What this covers

Companies with a 31 March financial year-end have two annual filings with the Registrar of Companies (ROC) that follow the annual general meeting (AGM): the financial statements (Form AOC-4) and the annual return (Form MGT-7, or MGT-7A for small companies and one person companies). This note summarises the timelines in the Companies Act, 2013 and how they work out for FY 2025-26.

The timelines under the Act

  • AGM: Section 96 requires an AGM within six months of the close of the financial year, which is 30 September 2026 for FY 2025-26.
  • Financial statements: Section 137 requires a copy of the financial statements, as adopted at the AGM, to be filed with the Registrar within thirty days of the AGM.
  • Annual return: Section 92(4) requires the annual return to be filed within sixty days of the AGM.

What the dates look like for FY 2025-26

If a company holds its AGM on the last permitted day, 30 September 2026, the AOC-4 falls due on 30 October 2026 and the MGT-7 on 29 November 2026. A company that holds its AGM earlier has correspondingly earlier dates, because both periods run from the date of the AGM and not from a fixed calendar date.

One person companies

One person companies do not hold an AGM. The Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 provide a separate timeline for them, linked to the end of the financial year. Their financial statements are generally due within 180 days of the close of the financial year, and they file MGT-7A.

Late filing

Filing after the due date attracts an additional fee under the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014, charged per day of delay. The Act also provides for penalties on the company and its officers in default under sections 92 and 137. Both the fee and the penalty amounts should be checked against the current text of the rules and the Act.

Practical points

  • Work out the dates from the actual AGM date, not from a general calendar.
  • Financial statements must be adopted at the AGM before AOC-4 is filed, so audit completion usually sets the pace.
  • MCA sometimes issues general circulars that relax or extend filing timelines or additional fees. Check the MCA website for any circular in force before relying on the dates above.
  • Category matters: the form (MGT-7 or MGT-7A) and some timelines depend on whether the company is a small company, a one person company or another category.
