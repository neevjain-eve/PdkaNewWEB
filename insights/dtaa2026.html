What Is the DTAA and Why Does It Matter?

India has Double Tax Avoidance Agreements (DTAAs) with 96 countries. These treaties cap Indian withholding tax (WHT) rates on payments to non-residents — typically well below the domestic rate of 20%–30%. For an MNC receiving Rs 10 crore in royalties from its Indian subsidiary, the difference between domestic WHT (20%) and treaty rate (10%) is Rs 1 crore per year in savings — and that compounds across all intercompany payments.

Key DTAA Withholding Rates by Country

  • USA: Dividends 25%, Royalties 15%, Interest 15%, FTS 15%
  • UK: Dividends 15%, Royalties 15%, Interest 15%, FTS 15%
  • Singapore: Dividends 15%, Royalties 15%, Interest 15%, FTS 10%
  • Japan: Dividends 10%/15%, Royalties 10%, Interest 10%, FTS 10%
  • Germany: Dividends 10%, Royalties 10%, Interest 10%, FTS: No FTS clause
  • Netherlands: Dividends 10%, Royalties 10%, Interest 10%
  • UAE: No DTAA — but India levies 0% WHT on dividends under domestic law since FY 2020-21
  • Mauritius: Dividends 5%/15% — subject to Principal Purpose Test (PPT)

How to Claim DTAA Benefits: Tax Residency Certificate

The non-resident recipient must provide a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from its home country tax authority plus Form 10F to the Indian payer before the payment. Without these, the Indian payer must deduct at the higher domestic rate. TRC must be valid for the financial year in which the payment is made.

Principal Purpose Test (PPT): The Anti-Treaty Shopping Rule

Since India ratified the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI), the PPT applies to most DTAAs. If one of the principal purposes of an arrangement is obtaining treaty benefits, those benefits can be denied. Key risk structures: routing royalties through Singapore/Netherlands holding companies with no genuine economic activity; Mauritius structures set up primarily for the 5% dividend rate without substance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How do I claim the lower DTAA withholding tax rate when paying royalties from India to a foreign parent?

A: The foreign parent must provide a valid Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from its home country tax authority and a completed Form 10F to the Indian payer before each payment. The Indian company then deducts TDS at the lower DTAA rate and reports this in the quarterly TDS return (Form 27Q). The CA signing Form 15CB for the remittance must certify the treaty rate is correctly applied.

Q: Is there a DTAA between India and UAE?

A: India and UAE do not have a fully comprehensive DTAA covering all income types. However, India levies 0% withholding tax on dividends paid to UAE residents under domestic law (post FY 2020-21 DDT abolition). Royalties and FTS payments from India to UAE residents attract the domestic rate of 20% since there is no treaty cap. Many UAE-based holding structures route India investments via Singapore or Netherlands to access their respective DTAAs.

Q: What is the withholding tax on dividends paid from an Indian company to a US parent?

A: Under the India-USA DTAA, dividends are taxed at 25% — higher than the domestic rate of 20% (post DDT abolition). So the India-USA DTAA actually provides no dividend withholding benefit. However, the domestic rate of 20% applies. The US parent can claim a foreign tax credit in the US for Indian WHT paid, reducing the overall double taxation burden.
