Why Bangalore Dominates India's GCC Landscape

Bangalore hosts over 500 GCCs employing more than 350,000 professionals — making it India's #1 GCC city by both count and talent. Major GCC corridors: Whitefield (tech), Electronic City (manufacturing IT), Manyata Tech Park (BFSI, tech), Outer Ring Road (tech, analytics), Sarjapur Road (emerging). Companies like Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, Boeing, Google, and Toyota all have significant GCCs in Bangalore.

Unique Finance and Compliance Challenges for Bangalore GCCs

  • Transfer Pricing: Cost + markup pricing to the foreign parent is scrutinised annually. CBDT compares the GCC's markup against comparable Indian ITES companies — typically 12%–18% for software, 8%–12% for BPO functions.
  • SEZ Compliance: Many Bangalore GCCs operate from SEZ premises (e.g., Embassy TechVillage, RMZ). Annual APR filing, NFE maintenance, and duty-free import documentation must be managed meticulously.
  • FEMA Reporting: Monthly service billing in foreign currency, annual FLA return, and any capital transactions (branch capital, intercompany loans) require timely FEMA filings.
  • GST Zero-rating: Services exported to the parent company are zero-rated — but the LUT must be renewed annually, and the export of services test (payment in foreign currency, service recipient outside India) must be documented.
  • Payroll Complexity: GCCs typically employ 50–5,000 employees with complex compensation structures (CTC, HRA, LTA, ESOP from foreign parent) requiring specialised payroll processing.
  • DPDPA: GCCs processing Indian employees' personal data and transferring it to foreign parent systems must comply with DPDPA's cross-border transfer restrictions and consent framework.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the best transfer pricing structure for a Bangalore GCC?

A: The standard TP structure for a Bangalore GCC is cost-plus: all operating costs (staff, rent, equipment, overhead) are recovered from the foreign parent at cost plus an arm's length markup. The markup is determined by benchmarking comparable Indian ITES companies using TNMM (Transactional Net Margin Method). CBDT typically accepts markups of 12%–18% for software development and 8%–12% for BPO functions. Lower markups attract scrutiny; PDKA models the optimal markup supported by the comparable set.

Q: Can a Bangalore GCC claim GST refunds on services provided to the foreign parent?

A: Yes. Services provided by a Bangalore GCC to its foreign parent are considered export of services (zero-rated supply) if: (1) the supplier is in India, (2) the recipient is outside India, (3) payment is received in foreign currency, and (4) the supplier and recipient are not establishments of the same legal entity. For GCCs (which are separate Indian legal entities), all four conditions are typically met. PDKA files GST refund claims for GCC clients to recover IGST on inputs used for the export services.
