What changed

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) notified the Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026 (Notification No. FEMA 23(R)/2026-RB, dated 13 January 2026). They replace the Foreign Exchange Management (Export of Goods & Services) Regulations, 2015 and came into force on 1 October 2026. The RBI's press release describes the framework as principle-based and aimed at ease of doing business, particularly for small exporters and importers.

On 22 September 2026 the RBI issued an amendment (Notification No. FEMA 23(R)/(1)/2026-RB, published in the Gazette on 24 September 2026). It shortened the time allowed to realise export proceeds and added two further provisions.

Who is affected

The Regulations apply to exporters of goods and services, including project exporters, to importers, to persons undertaking merchanting trade, and to Authorised Dealer banks, which carry out the monitoring and follow-up the Regulations describe.

Key provisions

  • Realisation period (Regulation 5): export proceeds for goods (from the date of shipment) and for services (from the date of invoice) are to be realised and repatriated within nine months. The January 2026 text said fifteen months; the 22 September 2026 amendment changed it to nine. Where exports are invoiced or settled in Indian rupees, the period is twelve months (earlier text: eighteen months). For project exports the period follows the contract's payment terms. An Authorised Dealer may extend the period on request if it accepts the reasons given.
  • Export declaration (Regulation 3): goods exporters submit an Export Declaration Form (EDF) stating the full export value; for exports through EDI ports it is treated as submitted with the shipping bill. Service exporters file an EDF within 30 days after the end of the month in which the invoice is raised, and one EDF can cover multiple recipients in that month. Non-software service exporters may file on or before receipt of payment.
  • Unrealised exports (Regulation 13): where proceeds remain unrealised for more than one year beyond the due date (or any extended period), further exports are allowed only against full advance payment or an irrevocable letter of credit. Exporters on the Caution List as of 30 September 2026 stay governed by the earlier order under the 2015 Regulations until they are removed from the list.
  • Merchanting trade (Regulation 16): the gap between the outward and inward remittances, in either order, must not exceed six months unless the Authorised Dealer extends it.
  • Transition (Regulation 20, inserted by the amendment): Authorised Dealers may handle export, import and merchanting transactions undertaken before 1 October 2026 that earlier required RBI approval.

Key dates

  • 13 January 2026: Regulations notified.
  • 22 September 2026: amendment notification issued (Gazette: 24 September 2026).
  • 1 October 2026: Regulations in force.

Practical points

  • Open export invoices and shipments should be checked against the nine-month period (twelve months for rupee-invoiced exports), as it is shorter than the period many exporters were used to under the earlier text.
  • Exporters of services can look at how their monthly invoicing lines up with the 30-day EDF timeline.
  • Merchanting traders may wish to track the six-month gap between the two legs of each transaction.
  • The Regulations are accompanied by separate RBI Directions on export and import of goods and services. The wording of the Regulations, the Directions and any later amendment is authoritative, and this summary does not replace it.
